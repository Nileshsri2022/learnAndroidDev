Here is a clear, structured breakdown of the short lecture covering Android Studio's
**Tip of the Day** dialog.

---

### **1. What It Is**

Every time you **restart Android Studio**, a small pop-up appears titled **"Tip of the
Day"**. It shows one productivity hint at a time — the example in the video is *"Create
Kotlin Projects"*.

---

### **2. Should You Dismiss It?**

The dialog has a **"Don't show this on startup"** checkbox, but the instructor recommends
**against** ticking it as a beginner:

* Android Studio can do a great deal that is **not obvious** from the interface.
* Clicking through the tips is a low-effort way to **discover features** you'd otherwise
  never find.
* Some tips will go over your head at first — **that is expected**. They become meaningful
  later in the course.
* You can also **rate** each tip as helpful or not.

Once you've had enough, just close the tab; it returns on the next restart until you
disable it.

---

### **3. The Second, More Practical Point**

The lecture also notes something easy to miss:

> If you **turn the emulator off**, you have to **restart it** before you can run anything.

In the video, the previously-running **My First App** reopens automatically with
Android Studio, and the **Device Manager** is used to start the AVD again when it isn't
already running.

---

### **Key Takeaways**

1. **Tip of the Day** appears on every Android Studio restart — click through it rather
   than disabling it while you're learning.
2. Expect some tips to be **advanced**; they'll click later.
3. A **closed emulator must be restarted** via Device Manager before the next run.
4. The running app may **reopen automatically** with the IDE.
