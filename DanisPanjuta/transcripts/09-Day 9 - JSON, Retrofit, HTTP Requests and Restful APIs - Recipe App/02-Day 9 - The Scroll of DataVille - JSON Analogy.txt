The scroll of data will in the kingdom of data will, there existed a vast library. The Grand librarian. The librarian housed scrolls upon scrolls of valuable information. These scrolls were written in different languages, used various symbols, and followed unique formats. While the knowledge contained within these scrolls was immense, diversity in formats made it challenging for scholars. For scholars from neighboring kingdoms to understand and decipher them. One day, a wise mage named Jackson visited Déterville. He noticed the scholars struggles in the Librarium, and decided to introduce a universal format that would simplify the exchange of information across kingdoms. This format was named Jason Jackson's Scroll of Notation. Jason had the following magical properties. Number one simplicity. Jason was easy to read and write. It used familiar symbols like curly braces for objects and square brackets for lists. Number two universality. Regardless of the original scrolls format, it could be transcribed into Jason, ensuring that anyone, anywhere could understand the content without needing a special translator. Number three. Compactness. Jason eliminated unnecessary details, making the scrolls lighter and easier to carry. And number four hierarchy. It allowed scribes to easily represent complex relationships and hierarchies using nested objects and lists. The scholars of Déterville were thrilled. They quickly adopted Jason and began transcribing their scrolls into this new format. Word spread, and soon kingdoms far and wide began using Jason to exchange information from trade agreements to magical recipes. Thanks to Jason, the flow of knowledge became smoother. Trade flourished. Collaborations increased, and the era of the Unified Scrolls began.
