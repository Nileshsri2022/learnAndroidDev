Now let's look at why a repository makes sense, because we are going to while we use repositories already, and we're going to go even deeper in the next few videos. So a repository is a design pattern that acts as a clean API for data access to the rest of the application. Think of it as a mediator between different data sources such as remote APIs, local databases, and in-memory caches. The benefits of using a repository are abstraction, so it provides a higher level abstraction over raw data sources, allowing you to switch between different data sources without changing the rest of your application. So, for example, you get the data from the web. Cool. And now you don't get the data from the web anymore, but you can get it from a database, for example. Well, cool. No problem. Just plug and play. As I said earlier, then centralized data handling. So all data related operations are centralized in the repository ensuring consistency and reducing redundancy. Number three is isolation. By isolating data related code in a repository, you can easily mock data for testing, change data storage mechanisms, or add caching layers without affecting the rest of your app. And number four is flexibility. If in the future you decide to change how you fetch or store your data e.g. for example, moving from one database solution to another, or adding a cloud based backup, you only have to update the repository without touching the view model or the view. Now let's look at a simple analogy. Imagine you're a chef in a restaurant, so the view the ingredients you need for your dishes can come from various places a farm, a local market, or even your own garden. These are the different data sources. Now, instead of going directly to these places every time you cook, you have a trusted supplier, the repository, who fetches the best ingredients for you. The supplier knows where to get each ingredient, when to harvest them, and how to store them all. You the chef. The view care about is receiving the right ingredients for your dish. The supplier abstracts away the complexity of sourcing ingredients, allowing you to focus on cooking. Similarly, in app development, the repository handles the complexity of data sourcing, letting the view model and the view focus on their primary responsibilities.
