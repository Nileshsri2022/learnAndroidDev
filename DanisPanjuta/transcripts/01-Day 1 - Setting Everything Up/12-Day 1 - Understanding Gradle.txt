Imagine you want to build a cool toy to show to your friends, and it could very well be an Android application, right? So you have a special workshop called Android Studio where you can design and create this cool toy. But before you can build, you need some parts like wheels, batteries, and so forth. In our case, it could very well be your user interface consisting of buttons, text fields, images, and so forth. And this workshop has a magical list. It's called Build.gradle, where you write down what you need and it gets them for you. And then once you have all of these parts, the workshops Magic Helper, which is Gradle, puts them together to create your toy. Before you can play with your toy, you want to make sure it works well. The magic helper checks it for you. So once everything looks good, the toy is all set. The toy is ready for you to play with, so your application is ready for you to use it or show to your friends and let them use it as well. So that's a general overview of what the Gradle does for us. It's going to be something we will look into a lot, a lot later in the course. I just wanted to give you a very, very brief introduction and an idea of what the Gradle is for, because otherwise you might be totally lost. What is this Gradle?
