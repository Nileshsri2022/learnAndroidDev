# The Kingdom of Appdom and the Royal Orchestra — MVVM

---

## The story

In the vast realm there is a grand palace known for its intricate architecture and harmonious
design. At its heart is the **Royal Orchestra**.

* **The monarch** = the **User**. They express a wish.
* **Victor, the ViewModel** = the **master conductor**. He interprets the monarch's wish and
  tells the orchestra what to play.
* **The orchestra** = the **App**.
* **The Models** = the backbone of the kingdom — they store the knowledge and secrets, i.e. the
  **data**.
* **The Views** = the instruments. Each instrument shows one part of the grand symphony, i.e. the
  **UI components**.
* **Victor's baton** = **events and commands**.

The conductor never plays an instrument himself, and the instruments never decide what to play.
Victor listens to the monarch, reads the sheet music (the Models), and makes sure the Views reflect
the current state of the Models.

That interplay is **MVVM**.

---

## The mapping, in one table

| Kingdom | MVVM | Real Android |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The monarch | **User** | Taps, typing, rotation |
| Victor (conductor) | **ViewModel** | `CounterViewModel` |
| The orchestra | The app | The whole application |
| The Models | **Model** | Data classes / repository |
| The Views | **View** | Composables in `MainActivity` |
| The baton | Events & commands | `onClick`, `onValueChange` |

---

## The one-directional flow

```
   User (monarch)
        │  event: "increment"
        ▼
   ViewModel (Victor)  ──reads/writes──►  Model (data)
        │  new state
        ▼
   View (instruments)   ──recomposes──►  what the user sees
```

The View never reaches past the ViewModel into the data, and the Model never touches the View.
Everything goes through the middle.

---

## Why the analogy is useful

It kills the two most common beginner mistakes:

1. **UI doing the thinking** — instruments that decide their own notes.
2. **Data knowing about the UI** — sheet music that knows which instrument is playing it.

Victor exists so that neither has to. That is the whole point of the extra class.
