# Lucy’s Infinite Bookshelf — What `LazyColumn` Is

---

## Overview

A story, then the real definition. You need this before putting shopping rows on screen.

---

## The story (short)

Lucy’s bookstore has an “infinite bookshelf.” It only shows the books in front of you. Scroll, and new books appear while the ones that left the viewport vanish. Never overcrowded.

Jamie (a coder) says: that is **`LazyColumn` in Jetpack Compose**.

---

## What `LazyColumn` actually does

A normal `Column` **composes every child** even if they are off-screen. A social feed with thousands of rows would run out of memory.

`LazyColumn` is a column that is **lazy**:

* Only items **visible** (plus a small buffer) are composed
* Scroll down → new items are composed
* Scroll away → off-screen items are **discarded**
* That is why Instagram / Twitter-style feeds can scroll “forever”

“Lazy” = do not do work until you have to.

---

## `Column` vs `LazyColumn`

| | `Column` | `LazyColumn` |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Children | All composed at once | Only visible ones |
| Good for | Small, fixed layouts | Long or growing lists |
| API | Nested composables | `items(list) { ... }` inside a lazy scope |
| Memory | Grows with list size | Stays roughly proportional to the screen |

Shopping lists can grow without a fixed max, so `LazyColumn` is the right tool — even if your first list only has three rows.
